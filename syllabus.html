Syllabus

Three weeks, one module per week. Each week lists what you should be able to do by the end of it and the work you are expected to turn in.

Week 1

How LLMs Generate Answers

Learning objectives

  • Define a large language model (LLM) and explain where the term “Artificial Intelligence” came from.
  • Explain how your words are broken into tokens, how an LLM predicts the next token, and how LLMs became helpful.
  • Explain why the answers an LLM predicts aren’t always correct.

Assignments

  • Read Module 1.
  • Activity: ask an AI chatbot the same question three times and write down how the answers differ.
  • Reflection (150 words): how did you think AI worked before this week, and what changed?
  • Complete Quiz 1.
Week 2

Recognizing Common Mistakes

Learning objectives

  • Recognize two common mistakes people make while using AI: over relying on it and weak prompting.
  • Describe real examples of AI giving false outputs.
  • Apply a simple routine for checking an AI answer against a source you already trust.

Assignments

  • Read Module 2.
  • Fact-check exercise: ask an AI a question about a topic you know well, then list anything it got wrong, left out, or could not support.
  • Complete Quiz 2.
Week 3

Realistic Concerns vs. Myths

Learning objectives

  • Recognize the two extreme positions people take on AI.
  • Correct four common myths about AI involving jobs, bias, creativity, and human oversight.
  • Separate realistic concerns about AI from the myths surrounding it.

Assignments

  • Read Module 3.
  • Myth or concern? Find one news headline about AI and write a paragraph explaining whether it describes a realistic concern, a myth, or some of both.
  • Complete Quiz 3.
