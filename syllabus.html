Syllabus

Five weeks, one module per week. Each week lists what you should be able to do by the end of it and the work you are expected to turn in.

Week 1

How LLMs Generate Answers

Learning objectives

  • Define artificial intelligence and large language model (LLM) in plain language.
  • Explain how your words become tokens, how an LLM predicts the next token, and how it learned to be helpful.
  • Explain why a fluent, confident answer is not automatically a correct one.

Assignments

  • Read Module 1.
  • Activity: ask an AI chatbot the same question three times and write down how the answers differ.
  • Reflection (150 words): how did you think AI worked before this week, and what changed?
  • Complete Quiz 1.
Week 2

Recognizing Common Mistakes

Learning objectives

  • Recognize two common mistakes people make with AI, over relying on it and weak prompting, and how AI itself can give wrong advice.
  • Describe warning signs that a picture, video, or piece of text may be AI generated.
  • Apply a simple routine for verifying an AI answer against a trusted source.

Assignments

  • Read Module 2.
  • Fact-check exercise: ask an AI a question about a topic you know well, then list anything it got wrong, left out, or could not support.
  • Complete Quiz 2.
Week 3

Realistic Concerns vs. Myths

Learning objectives

  • Tell the difference between concerns that are supported by evidence and myths driven by fear or hype.
  • Correct five common myths about AI involving jobs, bias, creativity, human oversight, and predicting the future.
  • Evaluate a claim about AI by asking who is making it and what evidence supports it.

Assignments

  • Read Module 3.
  • Myth or concern? Find one news headline about AI and write a paragraph explaining whether it describes a realistic concern, a myth, or some of both.
  • Complete Quiz 3.
Week 4

Using AI Responsibly at Work and School

Learning objectives

  • Find and follow the AI rules set by a class, school, or workplace.
  • Decide what information is safe to share with an AI tool and what is not.
  • Disclose and cite AI assistance honestly, and take responsibility for the final work.

Assignments

  • Read Module 4.
  • Policy check: look up the AI policy for one of your classes or your workplace and summarize what it allows and forbids.
  • Draft a short personal AI-use statement (5 to 7 sentences) describing how you will and will not use AI.
  • Complete Quiz 4.
Week 5

Deciding When to Use AI

Learning objectives

  • Judge whether a task is a good or poor fit for AI based on the stakes and how easily the result can be checked.
  • Recognize when using AI would get in the way of learning a skill yourself.
  • Make your own informed choice about AI instead of reacting from fear or hype.

Assignments

  • Read Module 5.
  • Final reflection (300 words): pick three tasks from your own life and explain whether you would use AI for each one and why.
  • Complete Quiz 5.
